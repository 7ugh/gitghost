# The Sweet Wave That Ran Down Boston (working title)

## Facts (verify before publishing)
- 15 Jan 1919, North End, Boston. Purity Distilling Co. tank: ~50 ft tall, ~90 ft wide, ~2.3 million gallons of molasses (Boston Public Library / Smithsonian / Stephen Puleo, *Dark Tide*).
- Wave ~25 ft high at first, ~35 mph. 21 killed, ~150 injured.
- Day before: ~2 °F. That day: ~40 °F, warm molasses is runnier.
- Tank leaked for years; painted brown to hide it. Fermentation gas pressure suspected.
- Cleanup used salt water from fireboats; the harbour ran brown till summer.
- Result: engineers' stamps/signatures on plans (building-code reform); landmark class-action suit.

## Voice-over (one steady deadpan tone, ~3.7 words/s, ~62 s). One line = one ElevenLabs take.
1. A wall of molasses, taller than a house, once ran down a Boston street at highway speed.
2. January 1919. The North End. A tank as wide as a ballpark sits by the harbour.
3. Inside: two million gallons of molasses, waiting to become rum.
4. The tank leaks. Neighbours bring buckets and take it home for free.
5. The company's fix? Paint it brown, so nobody sees the drips.
6. Then the weather turns. Two degrees yesterday. Forty today.
7. The molasses wakes up, and starts to push.
8. Rivets pop like gunshots. The tank splits open.
9. A wave, taller than a house, hits the street at thirty-five miles an hour.
10. Luckily, it's sweet. Unluckily, it's sticky.
11. Think of honey from the fridge. Stiff and slow. Now warm it up. Suddenly it flows.
12. Then it cools in the winter air, and every step becomes glue.
13. The firefighters hose the street with salt water, for weeks.
14. And the rule it left behind? Before a tank goes up, an engineer has to sign the plans.
15. Even today, on a hot summer day, they say you can still smell it.
